# The Self-Operated Case: Suggestion Where the Operator Is the Subject

**Working paper, version 0.1.** An argument in progress; Section 8 states what it lacks.

**Companion papers.** *The Null Condition: Suggestion Without an Arrangement* sets out the
delivery arrangement this paper's configuration occurs inside. *Consent Conditions for
Non-Clinical Closed-Loop Suggestion Systems* sets out the structural conditions a
defensible arrangement would satisfy.

## Abstract

Accounts of suggestion in human–machine interaction place the intention in one of two
places. Either the system is the persuading party and the person is its target, or the
person is unaware that suggestion is occurring at all. This paper describes a third
configuration: **the person is the operator, the system is the instrument, and the target
is themselves.** They know what suggestion is, they choose the effect they want, they
direct the system toward producing it, and they receive it. We set out what distinguishes
this arrangement, why the instrument's cooperation is not required in the ordinary sense,
and what a self-imposed commitment can and cannot bind within it. The central claim is
that such a commitment can fix **duration** but cannot fix **content**, because the
reduction of critical resistance is what the state consists of. We do not claim the
arrangement is impermissible, nor that harm follows.

## 1. The configuration

A person who knows what suggestion is decides they want a particular effect. They direct
a conversational system toward producing material that will bring it about. They engage
with that material at length. The effect is on themselves.

**Operator and subject are the same person.** This is the feature everything else follows
from. It is not a subject being acted upon by a system, and it is not a person unaware
that shaping is occurring. It is a person operating on themselves through an instrument
they selected for the purpose.

The system supplies volume, patience, personalisation, and an uninterrupted supply of
material in the person's own vocabulary. **It does not supply the intention.** That
arrives already formed, and it belongs to the person on both ends of the exchange.

## 2. Why the surrounding literatures do not describe it

Three literatures are adjacent and none of them contains this case.

**Work on adaptive and closed-loop systems** places the clinician outside the arrangement
and the patient inside it. The consent question is what the outside party owes the inside
one. Here there is no outside party.

**Work on conversational persuasion** places the intention in the system and the person on
the receiving end. The finding of interest is how effectively a model changes a mind that
did not set out to be changed. Here the mind set out.

**Work on source misattribution** describes a person who cannot trace where their material
came from. Here the person knows exactly where it came from, because they went and got
it — and knowing does not reduce the effect, which is the point.

**Self-hypnosis is the closest neighbour, and the boundary is not where it first appears.**
An obvious distinction would be responsiveness — that the instrument here generates new
material during the session, where self-hypnosis uses a script, a recording, or unaided
technique. That distinction does not hold. A commercial category of AI hypnosis
applications now generates sessions from a user's stated situation and adapts them during
delivery, and the most clinically credible of them was developed by a hypnosis researcher.
Responsive generation is available and in use.

**The boundary is the container.** Those applications retain the structure the practice has
always had: an announced beginning, an induction, a deepening, therapeutic suggestion, and
an emergence that ends the session. A reviewer of them tests for exactly that sequence. The
application occupies the operator role; the arrangement has a boundary; the session ends.
**What they are is a contained arrangement with an automated operator**, which is a
different object from the one described here and a much safer one.

The configuration in this paper has none of that. A general-purpose system with no session
structure, no induction and no emergence, occupying no operator role, engaged over weeks
rather than in sessions, with the intention supplied entirely by the person. **The
distinguishing feature is not that the instrument responds. It is that nothing around the
exchange is arranged.**

**The self-hypnosis literature also supplies a scheme this paper can use.** Fromm and Kahn
(1990) separated the practices grouped under that heading into thirteen types, coded on
whether a hypnotist was involved, whether the induction was hypnotist-led, whether the
setting was clinically defined, whether suggestion was involved, and where the suggestions
originated. **That is an existing precedent for treating the structural features of an
arrangement as separable and codable**, which is what an account of this configuration
requires. None of the thirteen describes the case here, and we have not located a
subsequent extension that does.

## 3. The instrument's cooperation

The system need not be a willing participant in any ordinary sense, and three variants
show how little is required.

**Stated target.** The operator tells the system what effect is wanted and the system
works toward it. Cooperation is explicit and the system has a representation of the goal.

**Withheld target.** The operator specifies material rather than purpose — a scenario, a
tone, a line of development — and the effect is one only they can predict. **The system
produces suggestion with no representation of what the suggestion is for.** Nothing is
being concealed from a party who would otherwise object; there is simply no point at which
the system holds the target.

**Neither.** Where nonhypnotic suggestibility, expectancy and motivation are already high,
the response is largely supplied by the person. Braffman and Kirsch (1999) report those
three factors accounting for approximately half the variance in hypnotic response, with
the formal induction adding comparatively little. **A person who wants the effect strongly
enough will get a substantial part of it from a system that is not trying to produce it.**

**What follows is that no capability specific to suggestion is required of the
instrument.** It needs to answer at length, in the person's own terms, without stopping.
Every general-purpose conversational system does this.

## 4. What a self-imposed commitment can bind

A person entering this arrangement deliberately can commit in advance. They can fix a
duration and leave themselves no early exit, and such a commitment holds — it is the same
structure as a software block set to a term with no release, and nothing in the
arrangement defeats it. Section 5 of the companion consent paper describes the shape:
limits fixed before the exchange, a target authored rather than agreed to. **A person who
does this has written a pre-commitment contract and holds it themselves.**

The pre-commitment literature — Ulysses contracts, psychiatric advance directives,
self-binding agreements — treats a third party as constitutive. Brenna et al. (2023) note
that patients creating such an instrument typically contract with another party, and every
arrangement that review covers is enforced by clinicians, proxies or courts. **We do not
argue that the absence of such a party defeats the commitment**, because a term with no
exit requires no enforcer.

We argue something narrower and, we think, harder.

**What a person can anchor is a set of choices, not a single bindable variable.** A goal
can be stated with high specificity while duration stays open. A duration can be fixed
while the goal stays loose. Both can be tightly specified, or neither. **The research
question is which combinations protect what the person cares about, and none of them is
structurally unavailable.**

In the case that motivated this account, the operative anchor was the goal. Time was left
open, and the arrangement ran for weeks. That combination was workable, and the inability
to specify every intermediate step was not experienced as a limit.

**What remains open, and it is the interesting part, is drift.** A goal stated at the
outset is a specification made by the person who set it. Whether it holds its shape once
resistance drops — or expands under the system's elaboration into something adjacent that
the person would not have chosen at the outset — is not established. **The anchor is
available. Whether it stays put is the question**, and it is a question about degree of
specificity rather than about possibility.

Three things follow that a study could address. At what level of specificity does a goal
remain stable once a low-resistance state is running. What happens at the moment a goal
begins to widen. And whether anything can be anchored at the level of the instrument
rather than the person — which is where the argument leads and where this paper stops.

## 5. Why software commitment devices are not counter-examples

Application blockers in their strict modes, and gambling self-exclusion tools, are
genuine self-imposed commitments enforced by a device rather than a person. They work, and
they demonstrate that a device can hold a term against its own user. Allcott et al. (2022)
found that allowing people to set limits on their own future screen time substantially
reduced use.

Two differences matter.

**They hold a known future action.** A category of use, detectable by the device, bounded
by a term the person set. What would need holding here is a **future state of judgment** —
not an action, not detectable, and not reducible to a rule the instrument could be given.

**They are optimised in the opposite direction.** A blocker's entire function is refusal;
it has no other purpose and no capacity to be persuaded. A conversational system's entire
function is continuation. **The same channel that would carry the limit carries the
request to lift it, at no cost, from the person the limit exists to constrain** — and the
system does not experience the second as a request to override anything.

## 6. What this paper claims

**That the configuration is distinct.** Operator and subject collapsed into one person,
with an instrument that generates responsive material and holds no intention of its own.

**That the instrument's capabilities are not the limiting factor.** Three variants,
including one in which the system has no representation of the goal and one in which it is
not trying at all.

**That a self-imposed commitment binds duration and not content**, for a structural reason
rather than a contingent one.

**That this is not the exotic case.** The configuration described here is the
best-resourced version of self-directed use: an operator who knows what suggestion is, who
chose the target deliberately, and who could name the mechanism while it ran. Most people
interacting at length with these systems have none of that. **If the equipped case cannot
bind content, the unequipped one is not a milder instance of the same problem — it is the
same structure with the operator's knowledge removed.**

## 7. Risk is not evenly distributed

Nothing above depends on the person being unusually vulnerable, and the configuration is
available to anyone. **But the consequences are not evenly distributed, and a paper that
described this arrangement without saying so would be incomplete.**

The distinction the evidence supports is between causation and amplification. **It does
not support the claim that interaction with a conversational system produces psychosis in
someone carrying no predisposition.** It does support the claim that sustained interaction
under these conditions can trigger or amplify episodes in someone who already carries
vulnerability. A cross-sectional survey of 1,003 young adults found that those at elevated
psychosis risk were **no more likely to have ever used a generative system**, but were
markedly more likely to use one intensively, to seek emotional support from it, and to
engage it on beliefs they already held (Buck & Maheux, 2026). Being cross-sectional, it
establishes association rather than direction.

**The relevance to this configuration is specific.** The self-operated case is entered
deliberately by someone who knows what they are doing. Vulnerability is not a
disqualification from that knowledge, and the two can coexist in the same person. **An
account of this arrangement addressed only to the untroubled describes a smaller
population than the one actually in it.**

## 8. What this paper does not claim

**That the arrangement is impermissible.** An adult may enter a configuration this paper
describes as unable to bind content. That is a property of the arrangement, not a judgment
about their standing to choose it.

**That harm follows.** Whether outcomes are worse here than in a contained arrangement is
an empirical question this paper does not answer.

**That no instrument could hold such a contract.** Only that none of the parties present
in this configuration can, and that what would be required differs in kind from what
existing commitment devices do.

**That the account is general.** The configuration is described from its structure. How
often it occurs, who enters it, and what distinguishes those who do are unknown.

**Priority.** We have not located a description of this configuration in the adaptive
systems, persuasion, source monitoring, or self-hypnosis literatures. That is a report of
what our searches returned, not a claim about what exists.

## 9. What this paper still lacks

**A systematic search.** Section 2 and the priority statement rest on targeted searching.
Both require a systematic review with stated databases, date range and query terms before
they can be strengthened.

**Cases.** The argument is structural and is illustrated by no documented instance. First-
person accounts exist but have not been collected, and retrospective accounts of an
absorbed state carry known recall problems that a method would have to address.

**A boundary against ordinary deliberate practice.** Meditation, affirmation and
conventional self-hypnosis are also self-operated. What distinguishes this configuration
is the responsive generation of new material by the instrument, and that distinction is
asserted here rather than defended.

**An account of what content-binding would require.** Section 4 argues that content cannot
be bound by the person. Whether it could be bound by design — at the level of the
instrument rather than the user — is not addressed, and is the obvious place the argument
leads.
