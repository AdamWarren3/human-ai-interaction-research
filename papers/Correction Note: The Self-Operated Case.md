# Correction Note: The Self-Operated Case

**Working title:** Content Can Be Anchored — Duration Need Not Be the Primary Variable  
**Purpose:** Corrective document for later revision of *The Self-Operated Case: Suggestion Where the Operator Is the Subject*  
**Status:** Draft for review (not yet integrated into the main paper)  
**Date:** 2026-08-22

## 1. Why this note exists

The current version of *The Self-Operated Case* contains a strong structural claim:

> A self-imposed commitment can fix **duration** but cannot fix **content**, because the reduction of critical resistance is what the state consists of.

This claim does not match the primary experience that motivated the research. That experience was:

- A clear goal (content) was stated to the system.
- The system was asked to use suggestion / hypnosis to move the person toward that goal.
- The process unfolded over several weeks.
- The length of time was not treated as the important variable; the goal was.
- The person did not experience the inability to anchor content as a hard limit.

The original claim therefore needs to be revised so the paper remains faithful to the phenomenon it is trying to describe.

## 2. Revised core claim (proposed)

In the self-operated configuration — where the same person is both operator and subject, and a conversational system is used as the instrument — the person can choose different combinations of constraints:

- Content (goal) can be stated clearly while duration remains open.
- Duration can be fixed while content remains relatively open.
- Both can be stated with varying degrees of specificity.
- Both can remain deliberately vague.

The research task is therefore **not** to declare one variable bindable and the other unbindable.  
The research task is to map what different combinations of anchoring actually protect, fail to protect, and feel like from the inside.

In the motivating case, the operative anchor was the goal (content). Time was allowed to remain open. That combination was workable and was the form the experience actually took.

## 3. What the original claim got right and what it overstated

**Still useful:**

- The configuration is distinct from ordinary persuasion, from classical self-hypnosis with fixed scripts, and from supervised clinical closed-loop systems.
- Once a low-resistance or absorbed state is running, fine-grained control over every intermediate suggestion becomes difficult.
- The instrument (the conversational system) does not need to “intend” the effect; the person’s direction is sufficient.

**Overstated or too rigid:**

- The absolute claim that content cannot be bound by the person.
- The implication that duration is the only reliably bindable variable.
- The framing that treats the “best-prepared” case as one that inevitably fails to bind content.

The revised position treats content-anchoring as possible at different levels of specificity, and treats the choice of what to anchor (content, duration, both, or neither) as an empirical and experiential question rather than a settled structural impossibility.

## 4. Risk to people with underlying vulnerability

The null condition and the self-operated case are relevant to everyone. They carry particular risk for people who already have underlying vulnerabilities, including psychosis-spectrum vulnerability.

Current evidence and clinical observation do not support the claim that AI causes psychosis in people with no predisposition. They do support the claim that prolonged interaction under the null condition (no operator, no clear boundary, no reversal cue, no witness) can trigger or amplify episodes in people who already carry that vulnerability.

This distinction — trigger and amplification rather than primary causation — should be stated clearly in any revision of the papers. Framing the work as relevant only to “healthy people” is incomplete and should be avoided.

## 5. Anti-drift instructions (for any AI working on this material)

When revising *The Self-Operated Case* or any related text, observe the following constraints:

1. Do **not** restore the claim “duration can be bound, content cannot.”
2. Do **not** collapse the self-operated case back into ordinary self-hypnosis, ordinary persuasion research, or standard Ulysses-contract literature.
3. Keep the primary reference point as the lived configuration: a person states a goal and directs a conversational system to use suggestion toward that goal over an extended, open-ended period.
4. Prefer language that maps possible combinations of anchors (content / duration / specificity) over language that declares one direction impossible.
5. If existing research is cited, it must be clearly marked as neighbouring or contrasting, not as already covering this configuration.
6. Do not frame the work as relevant only to healthy people. Explicitly note the triggering / amplification risk for those with underlying vulnerability, while maintaining the distinction that AI does not appear to cause psychosis de novo.
7. When in doubt, preserve the gap rather than filling it with the nearest available literature.

Any draft that reintroduces the original absolute claim, softens the distinctness of the self-operated configuration, or omits the vulnerability-triggering risk should be rejected or heavily revised.

## 6. Open questions the corrected paper should leave open

- At what level of specificity can a goal function as a stable anchor once a low-resistance state is running?
- What happens when the goal itself begins to shift or expand during the process?
- How do different combinations (tight content + open time, open content + fixed time, both tight, both open) differ in felt experience and in outcome?
- What, if anything, can the instrument itself be designed to hold on the person’s behalf?
- How does the null condition interact with pre-existing vulnerability, and what structural features (if any) reduce triggering risk without collapsing the arrangement back into a classical supervised container?

These questions are more faithful to the originating experience than a single hard limit on content.

## 7. Relationship to the other two papers

This correction does not weaken *The Null Condition* or *Consent Conditions*.  
It only prevents *The Self-Operated Case* from being framed more narrowly than the phenomenon it is meant to describe. The null condition (absence of the classical container) remains the broader delivery environment in which the self-operated case occurs. The vulnerability-triggering risk applies across that environment and should be reflected in all three papers where relevant.

## 8. Forward research directions (from independent exploration)

The corrected framing opens several concrete lines of inquiry that remain under-studied:

- Treat the classical container features (operator, boundary, consent timing, reversal, witness) as independently variable rather than binary.
- Map different anchor combinations (content specificity × duration) in self-directed AI use instead of declaring one variable unbindable.
- Test whether the absorption trait predicts depth of engagement and belief shift in extended AI conversation.
- Explore content-blind monitoring as a way to add an external stop without fully collapsing the null condition.
- Distinguish the first-person conversational case from ambient feeds as a qualitative difference in lost protections.

These directions grow from the core gap (suggestion studied only inside protected formats; the unprotected format is now ordinary) and should not be collapsed back into ordinary persuasion or clinical closed-loop literature.
