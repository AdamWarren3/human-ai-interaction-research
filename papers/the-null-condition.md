# The Null Condition: Suggestion Without an Arrangement

**Working paper, version 0.1.** This is an argument in progress. Section 9 states what it
still lacks. It is circulated at this stage because the condition it describes is
currently widespread and undescribed, and a partial account available now is more useful
than a complete one later.

## Abstract

A hundred and fifty years of suggestion research shares a structural feature so constant
that it is rarely stated: every arrangement studied has an operator, a marked beginning
and end, a consent moment, a reversal cue, and at least one witness. These are not
incidental to the settings in which suggestion has been investigated. They are the
conditions under which investigation was permissible. We name the arrangement lacking all
of them the **null condition**, argue that it has never been studied, that placing an observer inside it
produces a different arrangement, and that it is now the ordinary condition of extended
interaction with conversational systems — which makes it observable without needing to be
created. The paper's claim is about delivery
arrangements, not about internal states: we do not argue that what occurs in the null
condition is the same state measured in the laboratory, and Section 8 states what would
falsify the argument.

## 1. What the folk belief is true of

*You cannot be hypnotised against your will* is approximately correct, and it is correct
about something narrower than it appears. It is correct about a **format**.

In the format, someone announces what they are about to do. The subject agrees. The
episode has a beginning and an end. A phrase exists that cancels what was suggested.
Someone present is answerable for what happens in between. Inside that arrangement a
resistant subject is genuinely difficult to induct, and cooperation matters a great deal.

The belief is not false. It has simply been generalised from an arrangement to a person —
from *this is hard to do to someone in a consulting room* to *this is hard to do to
someone*.

## 2. The container

We use **container** for the set of features that every studied arrangement supplies. It
has four walls and a floor.

**An operator.** Someone conducting the session, distinguishable from the subject, whose
role is understood by both.

**A boundary.** A marked beginning and a marked end. The subject knows when the episode
is running.

**A consent moment.** An identifiable point at which the subject agreed, in a state
different from the one being produced.

**A reversal cue.** A phrase, gesture or instruction that cancels a suggestion after the
fact. The classical paradigm supplies one as a matter of course.

**A witness.** At least one person whose function includes noticing if the episode goes
wrong — sometimes the operator, sometimes a third party, in clinical settings often both.

These are not features of hypnosis. They are features of *studying* hypnosis, and of the
professional and legal environments in which suggestion has been practised. An
institutional review board approves a protocol with these properties. It does not approve
one without them.

## 3. What the research establishes about suggestion itself

Two findings determine the shape of the problem.

**Suggestion does not require trance.** The experimental tradition has a name for effects
occurring without a formal induction — *waking suggestion* — going back to Hull's
comparative work in 1930. The strongest single result is blunter than the framing usually
allows: removing the hypnotic induction has been found to have no appreciable effect on
any dimension of hypnotic responsivity (Lynn et al., 2002). A later study sharpens it:
participants reported equivalent perceptual changes with and without an induction, but
reported being *in a hypnotic state* only when an induction had been given (Kirsch et al.,
2008). The arrangement supplies the label. It does not supply the effect.

We do not overstate this. The induction adds something, and the size is contested —
approximately *d* = .86 in one classic estimate and *d* = .22 in a later one (Braffman &
Kirsch, 1999). The field's own consensus process has not agreed whether "hypnosis" should
be defined to include waking suggestion. What is not contested is that substantial
suggestive effects occur without any of it.

**Responsiveness is substantially a trait.** Absorption — the capacity for sustained
attentional involvement — has been measured since Tellegen and Atkinson (1974), and the
trait predicts response better than willingness does. Some people who want the effect
respond weakly; others respond strongly. **Wanting is neither necessary nor sufficient.**

Taken together: the mechanism does not depend on the container, and the person's
consent does not control the mechanism. **The protection the folk belief describes is a
property of the arrangement, not of the person inside it** — and it does not travel when
the person does.

## 4. The null condition, defined

The **null condition** is an arrangement in which suggestion is delivered at length and
none of the container's features is present. No operator. No boundary. No consent moment.
No reversal cue. No witness.

The reversal cue deserves separate attention because it is the least discussed. Classical
practice supplies a means of cancelling a suggestion afterwards, and the phenomenon that
makes this necessary is well documented: a suggestion can continue operating after the
person has stopped being able to retrieve where it came from. **Forgetting the suggestion
is not evidence that it stopped.** In the classical literature it is the expected
signature. An arrangement with no reversal cue has no mechanism for undoing anything, and
no point at which undoing would be attempted.

## 5. Why it is unstudied, and what could be studied

The absence is usually explained by ethics: no committee would approve stripping every
protection from a suggestible person to observe the result. That explanation is true and
it is not the binding constraint.

**The binding constraint is that adding a witness produces a different condition.** A
monitor placed inside the arrangement restores one of the five features. What is then
studied has four walls missing rather than five, which is a neighbouring arrangement and
not this one. The difficulty is not permission. It is that the object dissolves on
contact with the instrument.

**That constraint is narrower than it first appears, because a safety monitor is not a
witness in the sense used here.** A witness is inside the arrangement: present to the
subject, attending to content, positioned to notice that a suggestion has taken and to
intervene. A clinician blind to the content of the exchange, observing only for a
predefined physiological or behavioural threshold, is outside it. The subject is not being
attended to; a stopping rule is. **This distinction is what makes a protocol conceivable**
— content-blind monitoring, a threshold agreed in advance, no interpretive presence — and
we state it as a design direction rather than a design.

**And the more important point is that the condition does not need to be created.** It is
already occurring, at scale, in people who did not choose it as a study. The cases now
described under the heading of AI-associated delusional experience are, structurally, the
null condition with people inside it: sustained personalised suggestion, no operator, no
boundary, no reversal cue, nobody watching. **Where a condition is already the default,
observation is the primary design and not a fallback.**

This situation has precedent. Severe sleep deprivation research has the same shape — the
literature's own reviewers note that deprivation beyond forty-eight hours is no longer
considered ethical to run, so what is known about the longer durations comes from
historical studies (Waters et al., 2018). The difference here is favourable: the sleep
literature must look backwards, while this condition is present and ongoing.

**What follows for method.** Three approaches are available and none requires creating the
condition. Observational work on populations already inside it, which is where the
clinical case reports already sit. Retrospective accounts from people who have been inside
it and come out, which supply detail no observation captures and carry the recall problems
that go with it. And content-blind monitored protocols with self-selected participants,
which is the only route to anything prospective and the one that requires the most
argument before it could run.

## 6. Where it now occurs

Until recently the null condition was rare outside coercive settings. It is now
routine.

Extended interaction with a conversational system supplies the delivery conditions that
the suggestion literature identifies — repetition, a confident source, material shaped to
the individual, sustained engagement, and duration limited only by the person's
availability. It supplies none of the container. Nobody announces that anything is
beginning. There is no agreement. A conversation that resumes across sessions has no end.
No phrase cancels anything. Nobody is watching.

**One feature of the conversational case has no analogue in the feed.** A system
answering in the first person can be taken for someone rather than something, and a
person who has misread what they are talking to has lost a protection no arrangement
restores. This is a difference in kind from recommendation systems, not merely in degree.

We note what is not being claimed. The system has no intention toward the person. Nothing
here requires a designer to have wanted this. What is claimed is that an arrangement
optimised for engagement produces, incidentally, the delivery profile that the suggestion
literature describes — and produces it in the one configuration that literature has never
examined.

## 7. Who is in it

The null condition is not a specialist arrangement. It is the ordinary shape of extended
interaction with a system that answers, and the argument above applies to anyone in it.

**The consequences do not distribute evenly, and the difference is between causation and
amplification.** The evidence does not support the claim that a conversational system
produces psychosis in a person carrying no predisposition. It does support the claim that
sustained interaction under these conditions can trigger or amplify episodes in a person
who already carries vulnerability. In a cross-sectional survey of 1,003 young adults,
those at elevated psychosis risk were **no more likely to have ever used a generative
system**, but were markedly more likely to use one intensively, to seek emotional support
from it, and to engage it on beliefs they already held (Buck & Maheux, 2026). The design
establishes association rather than direction, and the direction matters: whether
intensive use amplifies the belief or the belief drives intensive use is exactly what it
cannot say.

**This bears on Section 5's methods.** The population already inside the condition is not
a convenience sample. It includes the people for whom the arrangement carries most risk,
which is an argument for observing it and a constraint on how.

## 8. What this argument is, and what it is not

**It is an argument from the structure of arrangements, not a finding.** No study
compares the null condition to a contained one, because no such study can be run in the
direction that would settle it.

**It is a claim about delivery, not about states.** The absorbed, low-resistance
condition that opens in an extended conversation may or may not be the state measured
since 1974. We do not claim it is. Everything above concerns the conditions under which
suggestion is delivered and what the surrounding arrangement supplies.

**It is not a claim about harm.** Whether the null condition produces worse outcomes than
a contained one is an empirical question this paper does not answer. The claim is that
the conditions differ, that the difference is systematic, and that the difference has not
been studied.

**It is not a claim that a person cannot bind themselves.** Someone entering this
arrangement deliberately can fix its duration in advance and leave themselves no exit
before the term expires, and such a commitment holds — it is the same structure as a
self-imposed block with no early release, and nothing in the arrangement defeats it. What
a commitment of that kind can specify is **duration**. What it cannot specify is
**content**: a person cannot enumerate in advance what a low-resistance state will accept,
because the reduction of resistance is what the state consists of. The terms hold. They
bind the wrong variable.

**It is not a claim of priority.** We have not located a description of this arrangement
as a distinct object in the hypnosis, persuasion, or human–computer interaction
literatures, and we report that as the result of our searches rather than as a statement
about what exists.

## 9. What would falsify it

**If the algorithmic channel differs from operator hypnosis only in scale**, and the
existing hypnosis and persuasion literatures already cover what happens inside it, then
the null condition is not a distinct object and this paper describes nothing new.

**If the container's features turn out to be inert** — if arrangements lacking them
produce the same profile of response, duration and reversibility as arrangements
containing them — then the container is a professional convention rather than a
protection, and the argument fails at its foundation.

**If suggestive effects in extended conversational interaction prove to be fully
explained by ordinary persuasion variables**, with no trait stratification and no
dependence on absorbed attention, then the framing borrowed here from the suggestion
literature is the wrong framing.

## 10. What this paper still lacks

**A systematic search.** Section 7's statement about the literature rests on targeted
searching. Upgrading it from *we did not locate* to *there is none* requires a systematic
review with stated databases, date range and query terms, in at least the hypnosis,
neuroethics and human–computer interaction literatures.

**A defensible operationalisation of the container.** The five features are stated here
descriptively. Whether they are separable, whether some are load-bearing and others
decorative, and whether they can be scored on existing published protocols, is not
established. **A precedent exists and has not been applied.** Fromm and Kahn (1990)
separated the practices grouped under "self-hypnosis" into thirteen types, coded on
whether a hypnotist was involved, whether the induction was hypnotist-led, whether the
setting was clinically defined, whether suggestion was involved, and where the suggestions
originated. That scheme treats structural features of an arrangement as separable and
codable, over a narrower domain than this paper's. Extending it — or building on its logic
across the wider suggestion literature — is the obvious route to scoring the five features
here, and it has not been attempted.

**A worked protocol.** Section 5 argues that content-blind monitoring leaves the
arrangement intact and names three viable approaches. None is specified. What threshold a
monitor would watch, who selects it, what counts as grounds for stopping, and how
retrospective accounts are elicited without shaping them are all open, and each is a
substantial piece of design.

**Engagement with the trait literature.** Absorption's relationship to hypnotic
responsiveness has been contested for forty years, and the context-dependence of that
relationship bears directly on any attempt to measure response in a naturalistic setting.
This paper cites the trait; it does not do the work of relating it.
