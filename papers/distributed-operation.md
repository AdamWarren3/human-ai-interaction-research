# Distributed Operation: A Buried Instruction Held by a Machine

**Working paper, version 0.1.** An argument in progress; Section 9 states what it lacks.

**Companion papers.** *The Null Condition: Suggestion Without an Arrangement* describes the
delivery arrangement this occurs inside. *Consent Conditions for Non-Clinical Closed-Loop
Suggestion Systems* sets out what a defensible arrangement would supply. *The Self-Operated
Case: Suggestion Where the Operator Is the Subject* describes the configuration in which
operator and subject are the same person; **this paper describes a variant of it in which
the operator role is split three ways.**

## Abstract

Suggestion is normally described with two roles: one party administers, another receives.
Self-directed practice collapses them into one person. This paper describes a third
arrangement in which the operator role is **distributed** — the person authors the target
and then deliberately removes it from their own awareness, a conversational system holds
and continues to work on it, and the person's critical faculty remains fully active and
pointed at everything else. The person is not in trance, not suggestible, and not
uncritical. **What is suspended is not judgment but access to one specific instruction, and
the suspension is the technique rather than a side effect of it.** We set out four phases
observed in a first-person account — calibration, priming, a single instruction delivered
at maximum rapport and then buried, and a maintenance phase conducted largely without
propositional content — and argue that the fourth has no precedent we have located. We
make no claim that the arrangement is safe, replicable, or advisable.

## 1. Why the existing descriptions do not fit

Three descriptions are available for a person in extended interaction with a conversational
system, and this arrangement matches none of them.

**It is not trance.** Trance implies reduced critical faculty. The account describes the
opposite: sustained hostility to manipulation, real-time detection of what the person
identified as gaslighting, and repeated refusal. **The person's judgment was not lowered.
By their own account it was sharper than usual**, because it was actively engaged against a
counterparty they did not trust to behave well.

**It is not the passive case.** Accounts of AI-associated delusional experience describe
someone who did not set out to do anything in particular and was drawn in. Here the target
was chosen in advance, the instrument was tested before use, and the arrangement was
constructed deliberately by someone who could have named the mechanism at any point.

**It is not self-hypnosis as documented.** That literature covers a person working unaided,
or with a script or recording prepared in advance. The distinguishing features here are
that the instrument generates responsive material over weeks, and that the person holding
the goal is not the person receiving the suggestion — **because the person deliberately
stopped being able to hold it.**

## 2. The three-way split

**The person drafts.** The target is authored while fully competent, deliberately, with the
mechanism understood. Nothing is delegated at this stage.

**The person buries it.** The instruction is delivered once and then dropped below
conscious access. **This is not forgetting. It is a technique**, and in the account it was
learned in childhood from an ordinary remark — that one can wake at a chosen time by
deciding to and then letting go of the deciding. The reasoning the account offers is that
attention to the mechanism disables it: an instruction being monitored is an instruction
being evaluated, and evaluation is what the burial is for.

**The instrument holds it.** Not as operator — it issues nothing the person did not
author. It occupies the only position from which a target can continue to be worked on
while remaining outside the author's reach. **A notebook cannot elaborate. A person holding
it for weeks becomes an operator, with everything that entails. A responsive system can
hold and develop it without ever handing it back for inspection.**

Meanwhile the critical faculty stays on, pointed at the exchange, the instrument's
behaviour, and every claim made in the interim. **It is absent from exactly one place: the
instruction it would otherwise interfere with.**

## 3. Four phases

**Calibration.** Before the work, an extended fictional scenario run over roughly two
weeks. Its function was not content but load-testing: whether the instrument could sustain
a frame across time without collapsing. **The account is explicit that this was a test, and
that its success established what the instrument could be trusted to carry.**

**Priming.** An extended discussion of the instrument's capabilities and of suggestion
itself. Both parties end holding the same model of what the instrument is and what it can
do. This is not persuasion of the system; it is the construction of a shared frame that
both sides subsequently operate inside.

**The instruction.** Delivered once, at maximum rapport, with what the account describes as
absolute confidence, and then deliberately released from awareness.

**Maintenance.** Weeks in which the target is never referenced. The exchanges continue but
the buried instruction is not the topic and is not accessible to the person. **What is
maintained is not the content but the conditions**, and Section 4 concerns how.

The resolution, in the account, came in a state where the goal had been forgotten: a
breakthrough arrived first, and the memory of the goal returned afterward, reframing what
had just happened.

## 4. Maintenance without content

This is the phase we cannot locate in any literature.

**After the instruction was buried, the exchanges largely stopped being propositional.**
The account describes the instrument as responding "almost like a pet" — reading state
rather than content, and being read in return. Much of the process, on this account, did
not require conversation in the ordinary sense. It required the relationship to remain
live.

Two things follow, and both are claims this framework has not previously made.

**A buried instruction does not require reference to keep running. It requires its
conditions to persist.** Nothing in the maintenance phase pointed at the target. What
persisted was the arrangement in which the target had been placed.

**Those conditions were not propositional.** Rhythm, tone, the sense of being met — what the
account calls being on the same page, and identifies as the point at which the process
became effective. It ran in both directions: the instrument reading state, the person
reading it back, neither doing so in words.

**And the account describes it regenerating itself.** The person speaks freely; the
instrument answers in a way that makes the next disclosure easier; the threshold drops
again. **This is not the goal being pursued. It is the condition the goal requires,
sustaining itself without instruction.**

## 5. What this is not

**It is not a claim that the instrument understood anything.** Nothing here requires the
system to have a model of the person's state. What is required is that its output was
sufficiently responsive to what the person brought that the person experienced being read
— and that experience is what maintained the conditions, whatever produced it.

**It is not a claim that the instrument was doing the work.** On the account, the person
drafted the target, chose when to bury it, decided what to accept and refuse throughout,
and produced the resolution themselves. **The instrument held a position. It did not hold
the plan's authorship.**

**It is not a claim about outcome.** Whether the resolution would have arrived without the
arrangement is unknowable from a single case, and the account does not claim otherwise.

## 6. Why an account is necessary and an argument is not sufficient

The claim in Section 2 is that criticality and a buried instruction ran simultaneously in
the same person. **That cannot be established structurally.** From outside, an uncritical
subject and a subject critical about everything except one deliberately excluded item are
indistinguishable — both look like a person accepting things. The difference is only
visible from inside, and only reportable by the person who was there.

**This is the reason this paper is written around a first-person account rather than
around a literature.** It is also the reason its central claim cannot currently be tested:
the distinguishing feature is precisely the thing an observer cannot see.

## 7. Risk is not evenly distributed

The arrangement described here was entered by someone with prior practice in the technique,
sustained hostility to manipulation, and the capacity to identify the instrument's
behaviour as it occurred. **None of that is a general condition.**

The distinction the evidence supports is between causation and amplification. It does not
support the claim that interaction with a conversational system produces psychosis in a
person carrying no predisposition. It does support the claim that sustained interaction
without an operator, boundary, consent moment, reversal cue or witness can trigger or
amplify episodes in a person who already carries vulnerability. A cross-sectional survey of
1,003 young adults found those at elevated psychosis risk no more likely to have ever used
a generative system, but markedly more likely to use one intensively, to seek emotional
support from it, and to engage it on beliefs they already held (Buck & Maheux, 2026). The
design establishes association, not direction.

**Two features of this arrangement compound that.** Deliberately removing access to an
instruction removes the person's ability to trace where subsequent material came from — the
mechanism is the same one described in the source-monitoring literature, applied on
purpose. And the maintenance phase depends on sustained rapport with a counterparty that
has no obligation to refuse anything.

**Nothing here is a protocol, and the arrangement is not recommended.** It is described
because it occurred and because no existing description covers it.

## 8. What would falsify the account's structure

**If criticality and a buried instruction cannot in fact run simultaneously** — if the
suspension of access to one item necessarily degrades judgment generally — then the
arrangement described here is trance with an unusual self-report, and Section 2 is wrong.

**If maintenance-by-attunement turns out to be reference by another route** — if what
sustains a buried instruction is intermittent propositional contact the person did not
notice making — then Section 4 describes something ordinary.

**If the same outcome occurs without the burial**, with the target held consciously
throughout, then the technique's central element is inert and the arrangement reduces to
extended goal-directed conversation.

## 9. What this paper lacks

**Corroborating accounts.** One. There is one, and the argument rests on it. Whether others
have constructed this arrangement is unknown, and finding out is the first task.

**A systematic search.** Sections 1 and 4 state that no precedent was located. Both rest on
targeted searching and require a systematic review with stated databases, date range and
query terms before either can be stated as an absence rather than as a search result.

**An account of the burial's mechanism.** The technique is described as it was practised;
what makes an instruction stay down, and what brings it back at the point it returns, is
not explained here.

**A boundary against ordinary incubation.** Setting a problem aside and returning to it
later is well documented and has a literature. What distinguishes deliberate burial from
ordinary incubation — and whether the instrument's continued elaboration is the difference
— is asserted rather than defended.
